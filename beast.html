The machine at a glance

A simple map of the main parts. Yours might be laid out a little differently, so match each number to the real thing next time you're standing in front of it.

A simplified front view. Not to scale.
  • 1Weight stacks. One each side, 120 kg each. The pin picks how many plates you lift.
  • 2Side pulleys. Slide up and down the upright with a pop pin. These are 2:1, so the stack number feels about half.
  • 3Overhead lat pulley. For lat pulldowns with the seat. 1:1, so it feels exactly as marked.
  • 4Pull up bar. Multi grip and sliding. Use the stepping block to reach it.
  • 5Smith bar. Runs on fixed rails. Twist your wrists to hook or unhook it on any notch.
  • 6Safety stops and spotter arms. Your built in spotter. Set them just below the lowest point of every move.
  • 7J-cups. Hold the free Olympic bar for squats and presses later on, outside the rails.
  • 8Lat seat and foot plate. Sit here for pulldowns. The foot plate lets you brace for seated rows.
  • 9Dip bar. For dips and knee raises. Start with your feet on the stepping block to help.
  • 10Landmine. A swivel sleeve at the base. One end of the Olympic bar goes in, you hold the other. Joint friendly arcs.
  • 11Storage. Pegs for plates and a peg board for attachments. Also: a leg press attachment for later.
How do I...

Tap a question to open the steps. Read them out to each other the first few times.

↕️Change a side pulley's height›
  1. Hold the pulley carriage with one hand so it can't drop.
  2. Pull out the pop pin (the round knob) and keep it pulled.
  3. Slide the carriage to the height you want: bottom for curls, chest for rows, head for face pulls, top for pushdowns.
  4. Let go of the pin and wiggle the carriage until you hear it click in. Tug it to check.
Change heights between sets, never while you're holding a loaded cable.
🎚️Change the weight on a stack›
  1. Make sure nobody is pulling on that cable and the stack is resting at the bottom.
  2. Pull the selector pin out of the stack.
  3. Push it all the way into the hole of the plate you want. Everything above that plate gets lifted.
  4. Check it's pushed in fully before you start.
Lightest pin first, always. Go up one plate at a time until 10 to 12 reps feel steady with about 4 more left in the tank.
⚖️Read the weight (the 2:1 thing)›
  1. Side pulleys (2:1): the cable runs through extra pulleys, which halves the load. Pin on 10 kg feels about 5 kg.
  2. Overhead lat pulley (1:1): no halving. Pin on 10 kg feels 10 kg.
  3. In the tracker, just write the number on the stack ("pin 10 kg"). It's the same every time, which is what matters for progress.
🔗Hook and unhook the Smith bar›
  1. Hold the bar with both hands, palms facing away.
  2. Rotate your wrists back (like revving a motorbike) to unhook it from the notch.
  3. Move it up or down on the rails.
  4. Rotate your wrists forward to hook it onto the nearest notch. Give it a tug to check it's locked.
Practise this with the empty bar until it feels automatic. Being able to rack it in a hurry is your main safety skill.
🛑Set the safety stops›
  1. Work out the lowest point of the move (for example, the bottom of your squat or your chest on a press).
  2. Set both safety stops on the same notch, just below that point.
  3. Do one slow rep with an empty bar to check the bar doesn't touch them before the bottom.
If a rep goes wrong, just sit the bar down on the stops and step away. That's what they're for.
💺Use the lat pulldown seat›
  1. Clip the long lat bar onto the overhead cable.
  2. Set the knee pad so your thighs are snug underneath it when your feet are flat.
  3. Stand, grab the bar wide, and sit down with it so the weight lifts.
  4. Pull the bar to your collarbone, elbows down and back. Let it rise slowly.
🎯Set up the landmine›
  1. Check the landmine sleeve is fitted and swivels freely.
  2. Slide one end of the empty Olympic bar all the way into the sleeve.
  3. Lift the other end and move it side to side to check it pivots smoothly.
  4. Only add a plate to the free end once the empty bar feels easy.
🏋️Load plates safely›
  1. Rack the bar first, on the J-cups or hooked on the Smith.
  2. Load both sides evenly, one plate at a time, alternating sides.
  3. Put a collar on each side before you lift.
  4. Unload the same way: alternate sides so the bar never tips.
The empty Olympic bar weighs 20 kg on its own. That's a real weight, so it counts as a workout.

Five safety rules

Before every machine set

  1. Lightest setting first. Earn every step up.
  2. Safety stops set, every Smith set.
  3. Pins pushed fully in, pop pins clicked.
  4. Change settings only when the cable is slack.
  5. Your partner watches your first reps of anything new.
